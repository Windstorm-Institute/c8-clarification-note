## Cover-letter abstract

A candidate covariant extension of the Gravitational Entropy Escrow framework — a local entropy-current equation `∇_μ J_S^μ = (2π k_B / ℏ) · ε_bind` (C8) emerging from AI-assisted exploration — is shown to reduce, under light-crossing-time integration, to the saturated Bekenstein bound (Bekenstein 1981). C8 reproduces Bekenstein–Hawking and Gibbons–Hawking entropies exactly, but only as the trivial consequence of horizons saturating the bound by construction. The choice of integration time is post-hoc, with natural alternatives (Hawking evaporation, inverse surface gravity, free-fall) yielding answers across tens of orders of magnitude. C8 is not a viable independent extension of the parent framework. The note also documents a methodology case study in which three of four AI systems consulted were confidently wrong at various points; the resolution required first-principles calculation against published Planck 2018 values rather than further LLM review. Companion to *Gravitational Entropy Escrow* (Whitmer 2026, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20032023).

## Why Entropy / MDPI

This paper sits squarely at the intersection of (i) entropic / emergent gravity (Bekenstein bound, Bekenstein–Hawking, Gibbons–Hawking), (ii) thermodynamic methodology (dimensional analysis, energy-density vs mass-density conventions), and (iii) the increasingly-relevant question of how to operate multi-LLM adversarial-review pipelines for technical work without recursion-to-error. Entropy regularly publishes both technical contributions and methodology case studies in this space.

The paper's posture is honest: it documents a *negative* result on a specific covariant extension proposal, and a *constructive* methodology lesson on multi-LLM workflows. Both are intended to save future researchers time.
