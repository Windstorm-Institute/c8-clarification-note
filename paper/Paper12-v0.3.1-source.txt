        On the Status of Local Entropy-Current Extensions
         of the Gravitational Entropy Escrow Framework: A
                          Clarification Note

                                      Grant L. Whitmer III
                            Windstorm Institute, Fort Ann, New York
                                    Draft v0.3.1 — May 2026

Abstract
In dialogue exploring possible covariant extensions of the Gravitational Entropy Escrow
framework (Whitmer 2026), several large-language-model systems independently proposed an
equation of the form ∇_μ J_S^μ = (2π k_B / ħ) · ε_bind, designated C8, in which a local entropy
4-current is sourced by the local Tolman-Komar binding-energy density. We document the
resolution of this proposal across multiple rounds of analysis. With dimensions accounted for
correctly, with the standard energy density of the cosmological constant ε_Λ = Λc⁴/(8πG), and
with the integration time chosen as the light-crossing time of the relevant horizon, C8
reproduces both the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy of a Schwarzschild black hole and the
Gibbons-Hawking entropy of the de Sitter cosmological horizon exactly. However, this recovery
is a consistency check rather than a derivation: C8 with t = R/c is algebraically identical to the
saturated Bekenstein bound (Bekenstein 1981), and any horizon that saturates the Bekenstein
bound by construction will be reproduced by C8 trivially. The choice of integration time t = R/c is
post-hoc; other natural time scales associated with the horizon (Hawking evaporation, inverse
surface gravity, free-fall) give different answers ranging across many orders of magnitude. C8
therefore reduces to a rate-equation reformulation of an established 1981 result rather than
producing genuinely new physics. We document this resolution — and the chain of dimensional
and convention errors that complicated reaching it — to spare future researchers extending the
escrow framework from the same dead end. The published paper's interpretive content is
unaffected; its central open problem (the order-unity coefficient α ≈ 1.34 between the bare Λ-
form a₀ and the empirical Milgrom value) is not addressed by C8 in either direction.

1. Motivation
The Gravitational Entropy Escrow framework (Whitmer 2026, henceforth W26) proposes that
gravitational binding energy is structurally identical to entropy held in escrow against the local
Unruh temperature: S_esc = |U_grav| / T, with the force law F = T · ∇S_esc recovering
Newton's law (Verlinde 2011). The framework recovers Bekenstein-Hawking entropy at
Schwarzschild horizons, the order of magnitude of the empirical Milgrom acceleration scale a₀
from a thermodynamic crossover at the de Sitter horizon, and several other results discussed in
W26. Section 7.6 of W26 identifies a complete derivation of modified field equations from a
variational principle on total entropy as the natural next step.
In dialogue with multiple large-language-model systems exploring such an extension, a
covariant equation of the form
                            ∇_μ J_S^μ = (2π k_B / ħ) · ε_bind     [C8]
was independently proposed, with J_S^μ an entropy 4-current and ε_bind the Tolman-Komar
binding-energy density. The dimensions balance: both sides have units of entropy production
density per unit time, k_B/(m³ s).
This note documents the resolution of C8's status. The result is small and specific: C8 with the
light-crossing-time integration prescription is algebraically identical to the saturated Bekenstein
bound (Bekenstein 1981). It therefore reproduces both the Bekenstein-Hawking and Gibbons-
Hawking entropies exactly when the standard energy density of the cosmological constant is
used. The recovery is a consistency check rather than a derivation, because horizons saturate
the Bekenstein bound by construction. The choice of integration time is post-hoc rather than
derived. C8 is therefore not a viable independent extension of W26; it is a rate-equation
reformulation of a 1981 result.
This note also documents, briefly, a sequence of errors made during the analysis. We include
them not because the errors are interesting in themselves but because they are easy to repeat,
and the methodological lesson is sharper than the technical one. The companion methodology
paper (in preparation) will treat this case study at greater length.

2. C8 as a Rate Equation
C8 is a continuity-like equation in which the divergence of an entropy 4-current is sourced by an
energy density. Both sides have dimensions of entropy production density per unit time. To
compare C8 to a total entropy (such as Bekenstein-Hawking or Gibbons-Hawking) one must
first integrate the rate over both volume and time.
Integrating C8 over a region of volume V containing total binding energy E gives the total
entropy production rate for the region:
                                      dS/dt = (2π k_B / ħ) · E
Multiplying by a characteristic time t_h yields a total entropy:
                                   S_C8 = (2π k_B / ħ) · E · t_h
The choice of t_h is the load-bearing question. It is not specified by C8 itself.

3. C8 with t = R/c is the Saturated Bekenstein Bound
Choosing t_h = R/c, where R is the radius of the region:
                       S_C8 = (2π k_B / ħ) · E · (R/c) = 2π k_B E R / (ħ c)
This is the Bekenstein bound (Bekenstein 1981), saturated. The bound applies as an inequality
to any localized system: the maximum entropy that a region of energy E and radius R can
contain in a quantum field theory respecting causality and the second law is 2π k_B E R / (ħ c).
Black hole event horizons saturate the bound, with the saturation value being the Bekenstein-
Hawking entropy. The de Sitter cosmological horizon also saturates the bound when the
appropriate energy is the total vacuum energy enclosed within the horizon.
Therefore C8 with light-crossing-time integration equals the saturated Bekenstein bound by
algebra, and reproduces horizon entropies for any horizon that saturates the bound. This is a
known relation written in a different form, not new physics.

4. Recovery of Horizon Entropies
4.1 Schwarzschild black hole
For a Schwarzschild black hole of mass M, taking the rest energy U = Mc² and the
Schwarzschild radius r_s = 2GM/c² as the relevant radius:
             S_C8(BH) = (2π k_B / ħ) · (Mc²) · (r_s/c) = 4π G M² k_B / (ħ c) = S_BH
The ratio S_C8(BH) / S_BH = 1 exactly, independent of M. This is verified numerically and
symbolically in the accompanying script.

4.2 de Sitter cosmological horizon
For the de Sitter horizon, the radius is R_dS = √(3/Λ), a length (units m). Using the standard
energy density of the cosmological constant:
                               ε_Λ = Λ c⁴ / (8π G)    [units: J/m³]
This is the energy density (≈5.3 × 10⁻¹⁰ J/m³ for our universe, in agreement with Planck 2018).
The closely related quantity Λc²/(8πG) is the equivalent mass density (≈6.0 × 10⁻²⁷ kg/m³) and
must not be substituted for the energy density in this calculation, as we discuss in Section 7.
The total vacuum energy enclosed within R_dS is E_total = ε_Λ · V_dS where V_dS =
(4π/3)R_dS³. Then:
        S_C8(dS) = (2π k_B / ħ) · (ε_Λ V_dS) · (R_dS/c) = 3π c³ k_B / (G ħ Λ) = S_GH
The ratio S_C8(dS) / S_GH = 1 exactly, independent of Λ. This is verified in the accompanying
script.

5. The Light-Crossing Time is Post-Hoc
The choice of t_h = R/c is not derived from C8. It is one of several time scales naturally
associated with a horizon. For a Schwarzschild black hole of solar mass, alternative natural
choices include:
    • Light-crossing time r_s/c ≈ 10⁻⁵ s, giving ratio S_C8/S_BH = 1.
    • Inverse surface gravity c/κ ≈ 2 × 10⁻⁵ s, giving ratio = 2.
    • π r_s/c (related to the inverse Hawking frequency) ≈ 3 × 10⁻⁵ s, giving ratio = π.
    • Hawking evaporation time τ_evap ≈ 10⁶⁴ yr, giving ratio ≈ 10⁸⁰.
Only the light-crossing time produces ratio = 1, and it does so because R/c is precisely the
factor needed for C8 with prefactor (2π k_B / ħ) to algebraically equal the Bekenstein bound 2π
k_B E R/(ħ c). The other natural time scales — each defensible on its own physical grounds —
give different answers ranging across many orders of magnitude.
This is the textbook signature of a post-hoc choice. A genuine derivation would either select t_h
from a deeper principle (e.g., the Raychaudhuri equation, null geodesic focusing, or detailed
balance on the horizon) or would survive without privileging any particular choice. C8 with R/c
does neither. The agreement with horizon entropies that R/c produces is therefore a
consequence of the algebraic identity between (2π k_B/ħ) · E · (R/c) and the Bekenstein bound,
not independent evidence for C8.

6. Status and Implications
C8 with the light-crossing-time prescription is algebraically equivalent to the saturated
Bekenstein bound. It therefore reproduces Bekenstein-Hawking and Gibbons-Hawking
entropies for any horizon that saturates the bound, but this is a consistency check on
dimensional structure rather than independent confirmation of C8 as a covariant extension of
the escrow framework. C8 contains no information beyond what is already in Bekenstein 1981
plus the choice of integration time.
The published escrow framework (W26) is unaffected by this analysis. Its interpretive content
does not depend on C8. Specifically:
    • The static escrow postulate S_esc = |U_grav|/T at any configuration is unaffected.
    • The recovery of Newton's law via Verlinde's identity is unaffected.
   •    The recovery of Bekenstein-Hawking entropy at horizons (W26 Section 5) is unaffected;
        that recovery is independent of C8 and follows from substituting the Schwarzschild
        surface gravity into the static escrow expression.
    • The identification of the deep-MOND limit with a thermodynamic crossover at the de
        Sitter horizon (W26 Section 6) is unaffected.
    • The empirical tests reported in W26 Sections 6.1, 8.4, 8.5, 8.6 (the Genzel five-case
        test, the SPARC reanalysis, the baryonic Tully-Fisher relation, and cross-redshift
        consistency) are all unaffected because they depend on the deep-MOND asymptote a₀
        = c²√(Λ/3)/(2π), not on C8.
What is ruled out by this analysis is the natural-looking local-current generalization of the static
escrow postulate as a candidate independent extension of W26. Future researchers seeking a
covariant extension of W26 should be aware that this direction does not yield new content
beyond the static postulate plus Bekenstein 1981.
Three directions for future extension of W26 are not ruled out by this analysis:
    • Quantum corrections to horizon temperatures. The leading-order Gibbons-Hawking and
        Hawking temperatures may receive corrections from entanglement-entropy structure
        across horizons, which depend on the cosmological matter content (Ω_m) or other
        parameters. Such corrections might derive the order-unity coefficient α ≈ 1.34 named as
        the open problem in W26 Section 8.4.3. Calculations of this type have been done in
        lower-dimensional analogs (Calabrese-Cardy 2004) but not, to our knowledge, for the
        four-dimensional de Sitter horizon in a universe with realistic matter content.
    • Rindler–de Sitter horizon matching. The framework's commitment to constant a₀ (W26
        Section 6) requires the de Sitter horizon temperature to set the floor for entropy
        exchange of accelerated observers within the universe. The matching condition between
        local Rindler horizons and the global de Sitter horizon is a structural problem the existing
        paper does not resolve; a correct treatment would not be a local continuity equation.
    • Lattice quantum field theory tests of the static escrow postulate. The identification S_esc
        = |U_grav|/T can be tested directly by computing the entanglement entropy across a
        boundary separating two localized mass distributions in a quantum field theory on a
        lattice and checking whether the result scales as |U_grav|/T_Unruh. This is
        computationally tractable in 1+1D using Peschel's formula for Gaussian states (Peschel
        2003) and remains a sharp falsification test of the static postulate.
The framework's central open derivation problem (α ≈ 1.34) lives in the deep-MOND
interpolation, not in horizon entropy magnitudes. This note's analysis of C8 does not address it
in either direction.

7. Methodological Notes
This paper is the third draft of an analysis of C8. Two earlier drafts reported wrong results for
opposite reasons. We document the chain of errors so that future researchers — human or AI
— can recognize the failure modes.
The first draft (v0.1) reported that C8 fails by 30 orders of magnitude when applied to galactic
systems and by 4.7 orders of magnitude for solar-mass black holes. This was a dimensional
category error: the C8 source term (an entropy production rate density) was compared directly
to total entropies. The category error was not detectable from numerical magnitudes alone
because the apparent failure (30 orders of magnitude) was large enough to seem qualitatively
diagnostic regardless of dimensional details. The error would have been caught immediately by
a sympy-based dimensional check, which we did not perform on the first pass.
The second draft (v0.2) corrected the rate-vs-total error and re-evaluated C8 with t = R/c
integration. It reported that C8 reproduces Bekenstein-Hawking exactly but underpredicts
Gibbons-Hawking by 1/c² ≈ 10⁻¹⁷. This was a different kind of error: the calculation used the
mass density Λc²/(8πG) where the energy density Λc⁴/(8πG) was required. The two differ by
exactly c², which propagates as a factor of c² in the de Sitter total energy and therefore in S_C8,
producing a spurious "underprediction by c²" result. Symbolic dimensional analysis cannot catch
this kind of error because both forms are dimensionally consistent within their respective
conventions; only physical identification ("this should be an energy density, not a mass density,
because we are computing a total energy not a total mass") catches it.
The third draft (this document) compares each computed quantity against a published reality-
check value before treating it as established. The dark energy density ε_Λ = 5.3 × 10⁻¹⁰ J/m³ is
published in Planck 2018; the de Sitter horizon radius R_dS ≈ 16 Gly is published in standard
cosmology references; the Gibbons-Hawking entropy S_GH ≈ 10¹²² k_B for our universe is
published in horizon-entropy reviews. Computing each from first principles and comparing
against the published value catches convention errors that dimensional analysis does not.
After Perplexity's audit identified the mass-vs-energy density error, a fourth round of review was
sought from xAI Grok in the form of an “audit of the audit.” Grok's response confidently rejected
Perplexity's correction and asserted that Λc²/(8πG) is the correct energy density of the
cosmological constant in SI units, attributing the disagreement to Perplexity having confused
mass and energy density conventions. This is the opposite of what dimensional analysis and the
published Planck 2018 values show: Λc²/(8πG) yields 5.93 × 10⁻²⁷ kg/m³ (matching the
published mass density) and Λc⁴/(8πG) yields 5.33 × 10⁻¹⁰ J/m³ (matching the published energy
density). Grok's audit was confidently wrong in the same direction that the v0.2 draft had been
wrong. Resolving the disagreement required independent first-principles calculation against
three sources: dimensional analysis from the SI unit definitions, comparison against Planck
2018 dark-energy values, and cross-check via the Friedmann equation H² = Λc²/3 (SI form). All
three confirmed Perplexity's correction.
Three methodological lessons follow:
    • Symbolic dimensional analysis is necessary but not sufficient. It catches dimensional
        inconsistency within an assumed convention; it does not catch errors in choosing the
        right convention. The factor of c² between mass density and energy density of the
        cosmological constant is a case in point: both are dimensionally consistent within their
        own conventions, but only one is correct for a given physical question.
    • Reality checks against published values are necessary. Each derived quantity should be
        cross-validated numerically against a published value from an independent source
        before being treated as established. This is the check that catches convention errors and
        unit conversions that pure mathematics cannot.
    • Multi-LLM adversarial review is load-bearing but cannot resolve disagreements among
        LLMs. When two LLM systems give opposite verdicts on a technical question, the
        resolution must come from first-principles calculation against external published values,
        not from a third LLM. In the present case, three of the four systems consulted (Claude,
        Grok, and an earlier Gemini round) were confidently wrong about the c² convention at
        various points; only Perplexity caught the actual error correctly, and Grok's subsequent
        “audit of the audit” reversed the correct result back to the wrong answer with high
        confidence. Recursion of LLM review without external grounding is unstable.
Each of these lessons emerged from this specific analysis. The pattern across rounds —
confidently wrong, then confidently right, then confidently wrong again — is characteristic of
working with dimensional and convention questions in AI dialogue without consistent external
anchoring. The methodology paper in preparation will treat this case study at greater length.

8. Conclusion
C8 is the saturated Bekenstein bound (Bekenstein 1981) expressed as a local rate equation.
With the light-crossing-time integration prescription and the standard energy density of the
cosmological constant, it reproduces Bekenstein-Hawking and Gibbons-Hawking entropies
exactly. This recovery is a consistency check rather than a derivation, because both horizons
saturate the Bekenstein bound by construction. The choice of integration time is post-hoc; other
natural choices give different answers across many orders of magnitude. C8 therefore does not
constitute an independent covariant extension of the escrow framework, even though it is
internally consistent.
The published escrow framework (Whitmer 2026) is unaffected by this result. Its interpretive
content stands; its central open problem (α ≈ 1.34 in the deep-MOND interpolation) is not
addressed. Future researchers extending the framework should be aware that the natural-
looking local-current generalization reduces to a known 1981 result and should pursue
extensions through quantum corrections to horizon temperatures, Rindler–de Sitter matching, or
lattice QFT tests of the static postulate, none of which are ruled out by this analysis.
